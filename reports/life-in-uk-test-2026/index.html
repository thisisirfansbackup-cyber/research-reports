01Your test in numbersThe rules that decide your result

The Life in the UK test is a computer-based test with 24 multiple-choice questions. You get 45 minutes, and you must answer 18 correctly (75%) to pass.

Where the questions come from. Every real test question is drawn from the official handbook, Life in the United Kingdom: A Guide for New Residents (third edition). The Home Office does not publish the question bank, so this guide measures which facts appear again and again across the practice banks that thousands of candidates use.

  • Pass mark. You need 18 of 24 correct, which is 75%.
  • Time. 45 minutes for 24 questions, so about 2 minutes per question. Almost everyone finishes.
  • Language. The test is in English. You can request Welsh or Scottish Gaelic.
  • Booking. Book online at gov.uk for an official test centre. There are around 60 centres.
  • On the day. Take original ID and proof of address. Without them you cannot sit the test.
  • If you fail. You must wait 7 days before retaking, and you pay the £50 fee again.
  • If you pass. The pass never expires.
Important

Only the official test at gov.uk counts. A certificate from any other place will be rejected. The handbook and this guide are all you need.

Honesty note

The Home Office does not publish the real question bank, so no one can guarantee the exact questions. The 62 facts below are ranked by how often they appear across the 11 largest practice banks, weighted by how much space each chapter takes in the handbook. Some counts were adjusted by hand where a question is worded so differently between banks that a simple text search would miss it. If you know all 62 facts, you cover almost everything the real test can ask.

08How to studyA plan for the days before 3 September

You do not need to memorise the whole handbook. With 20 days left, a focused plan is enough.

  • First week: Tiers 1 and 2. Learn questions 1 to 29 until you can answer all of them without looking. This alone covers most of the test.
  • Second week: Tier 3. Add questions 30 to 49. Read the explanations, not just the answers, because the real test often changes the wording.
  • Final days: Tier 4 plus weak spots. Learn questions 50 to 62, then retest yourself on the ones you keep getting wrong.
  • Read the handbook once. Even a quick read of the six chapters makes the answers stick, because the questions come from it directly.
  • Do timed practice. Answer 24 questions in 45 minutes. It trains you to keep moving.
  • The night before. Sleep. The pass mark is only 75%, and Tiers 1 and 2 cover most of what will appear.
