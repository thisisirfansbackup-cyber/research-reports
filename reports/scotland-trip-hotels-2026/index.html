1. Why Four Adults Cannot Share One Room

The brief was “the cheapest hotel for 4 people”, which implies one room for four adults. That room does not exist in the UK budget market, and this is the fact that changes the whole answer. Verified from the operators' own booking widgets:

ChainMax adults in one room (booking UI)What a “family room” actually is
Travelodge3 adults — the selector stops thereRooms for 4 people exist, but built for 2 adults + 2 children
Premier Inn2 adultsFamily rooms are 2 adults + 2 children
easyHotel2 adultsNo 4-person room

So four adults means one of exactly four things: two chain rooms (usually the cheapest secure option), a hostel private 4-bed room, a shared dorm of four beds (cheapest of all, strangers in the room), or a 4-person serviced apartment. Everything below is costed as two rooms, because two rooms is what the chains will actually sell you.

Practical read: “a hotel room for four mates” costs roughly double a hotel room for two. Any comparison quoting single-room prices for this trip is wrong by a factor of two.

4. Sleeping in Edinburgh or Glasgow

There is a real trade here, and it is not only about money. Sleeping in the corridor means you see both cities but spend the Saturday night outside both. If a night out in Edinburgh or Glasgow is the point of the trip, pay the premium — knowingly. Cheapest sensible options on each side, four adults, Saturday night:

Edinburgh side

  • Edinburgh Airport (Ratho Station) — £279.98, free parking. West of the city on the M8 corridor; good for arriving and leaving, and for getting into town without centre parking.
  • Edinburgh Dreghorn — £279.98, free parking. South-west, handy for the A720 bypass; family rooms only at that price.
  • Edinburgh Park — £305.98, free parking, tram stop at the door. The best of the “still feels like Edinburgh” options, and the one I would pick if the Saturday night out matters.
  • Edinburgh Haymarket — £391.98 plus paid parking nearby. Walking distance to the West End; you are now paying ~£190 more than Falkirk purely for location.
  • Central Edinburgh (Waterloo Place / Queen Street / Central / Rose Street) — £459.98 to £599.98, plus £33–£36 a day parking. This is the “obvious” answer to your question, and the most expensive line in this report.

Glasgow side

  • Glasgow Braehead — £277.98, free parking. West of the city, retail park, easy M8 access.

7. Before You Set Off

The hotel is the small part of this trip's risk. These are the items that cost more than the room if they go wrong.

Low Emission Zones — check the car first (unverified)

Both Edinburgh and Glasgow operate Low Emission Zones over their city centres, with penalties that double on repeat offences. I could not verify the current emission standards or penalty amounts from here, so treat this as a to-do: check your registration against both city LEZ checkers before you drive in. One avoidable penalty can equal the entire saving you just made on the hotel.

Centre parking is the hidden second bill

Central Edinburgh hotels either have no parking or charge for it — earlier research noted Q-Park Capital Square at £33/24h and NCP Castle Terrace at £35.95/5–24h, both with height limits (1.95 m and 2.08 m) worth checking against a large car. Free on-site parking is worth £20–£35 a night, which is precisely why the corridor wins.

Leave the car at the hotel and take the train (verify timetable)

Falkirk sits on the Edinburgh–Glasgow rail corridor (Falkirk High, Falkirk Grahamston). If that holds for your times, you can park free at the hotel and travel into either city centre by train — which removes city parking, keeps you out of both LEZs entirely, and means you can drink. Verify times and last trains before relying on it.

Insurance, not driving ability

“All of them are drivers” is not the same as “all of them are insured”. On most UK policies only named drivers are covered, and “driving other cars” cover almost never extends to friends borrowing your car. Either name every driver on the policy or agree one driver and split the fuel. An uninsured 400-plus-mile round trip is the largest single risk in this plan, and it dwarfs a £42 weekday saving.

Four drivers and a Saturday night

If you go out in either city on the Saturday, one of you is the designated driver — or you train in from Falkirk. Decide which before the first round, not after.

Book direct, take the non-refundable, skip the breakfast

  • The cheapest rate class in the feed is the saver/non-refundable type, which is the right choice when the trip is four days away.
  • Book on the operator's own site: OTAs load commission into the rate, and Premier Inn does not appear there anyway.
  • Breakfast is excluded by your own choice, and the saver rates do not include it. Travelodge's unlimited breakfast is around £10 per person, so four of you would add roughly £40 — comparable to, or more than, the Friday/Saturday gap at some hotels. Buy it in town.

8. Method & Limitations

How the prices were obtained

  • Prices come from Travelodge's own Best Price Finder price feed (the S3-hosted grid page and the CloudFront JSON endpoints it calls), queried on 15 September 2026 for the week of 15–21 September 2026.
  • For each hotel and night the feed returns a price per room type; the figures quoted are the cheapest available rate per room, excluding entries flagged as stop-sell.
  • Destination groups pulled: edinburgh, glasgow, falkirk, west-lothian, stirling, fife, dunfermline, dumbarton, kinross and scotland, deduplicated by hotel name.
  • Date check: date -d '2026-09-19' confirms Saturday, and the research was run on Tuesday 15 September 2026 — four days before the night.

Assumptions

  • “Total for 4” = 2 × the cheapest room rate, i.e. two rooms of two adults. That is the only configuration the booking engines will accept for four adults, and the cheapest one.
  • Prices are per room per night, room-only, as quoted by the operator.
  • The earlier research notes flagged an ambiguity about whether Travelodge's “£98” figure was per room or a total. This feed resolves it: it is per room, so the four-adult total is roughly double.

What I could not verify

  • Premier Inn prices for Saturday 19 September — its site blocked automated access at the protocol level and its pricing is GraphQL-only. The Premier Inn figures quoted in section 6 come from an earlier manual scrape and cover Friday 18 only.
  • Hostel prices for the specific date — Hostelworld ignored date parameters in this environment, and Booking.com refused to return results at all.
  • LEZ standards and penalties, train times, drive times and mileage, and specific car-park tariffs — these are listed in section 7 as things to check, not as assertions.
  • Live availability at the moment you book — this is four days out on a Saturday, and the cheap rooms are the ones that go first.
